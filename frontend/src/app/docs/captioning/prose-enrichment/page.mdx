# Prose enrichment

<ConceptBody id="prose_enrichment" />

## What actually gets appended

Two captioners look at each image: WD14 produces tags, Florence-2 produces a
sentence. Enrichment mines that sentence for phrases the tags *missed* and appends
only those. Toggle it:

<ProseEnrichmentDemo />

The two rules worth remembering:

1. **Novel only.** A phrase the tags already carry is dropped, so enrichment never
   double-weights a concept it merely restated.
2. **Appended last.** The new tokens land at the bottom of the priority order, so a
   tight [token budget](/docs/captioning/target-backend) spends them first — they
   can never displace identity or wardrobe tags.

## When to turn it off

Leave it on by default. Turn it off when:

- You want a **pure WD14 tag caption** — reproducing a tag-only dataset, or
  debugging which captioner is responsible for an artifact.
- You are training on a **~60-token backend** and want the budget spent entirely on
  concept tags. (Though you can leave it on: the appended tokens are the first to
  be dropped anyway, so they cost you nothing you would have kept.)

## Where it shows up

**Prose Enrichment** in the [Caption tool](/) applies to folder, Immich, and manifest
runs. It is not accepted by the upload endpoint, so the toggle hides in **Upload**
mode.

Do not confuse it with the **tag ↔ prose balance** control next to it: balance decides
how much prose survives *into the prose variant*, while enrichment decides whether
novel prose phrases are appended *to the training variant*.

## Try it

- Open the [Caption tool](/) and expand the **Prose Enrichment** info panel — it
  reads from the same source as this page.
- Caption a folder with it on and off, then diff `final_caption` against `raw_prose`
  in the [raw outputs](/) panel to see exactly which phrases were mined.
